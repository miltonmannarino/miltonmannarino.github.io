Three weeks of work, in plain words

What Milton built between 15 October and 5 November 2026, what you can already see in the app, and what is still waiting for a go.

As of 5 November 2026 Written for colleagues, not for developers Repo: Lumoview-Building-Analytics-GmbH/platform (plus api, types)

The short version. Three things got visibly better for people who use LumoCad. The Room Book can now be handed to a customer as a branded PDF or Excel, with living areas computed by the German rules. Floor plan sheets need fewer manual steps: one click cuts a sheet for every flat, title blocks are readable on every sheet type, and facades draw clean. Stairs now cut openings in the floor that follow their real shape, and each stair can be built one of four ways.

Underneath, two pieces of automation are finished but not yet switched on: drone roof models from Airteam are fetched and matched to buildings on their own, and those models are read into roofs far more faithfully. The last week also laid the groundwork for an assistant inside LumoCad, and the team agreed to refocus on 2D automation.

1Room Book: a customer-ready Raumbuch in one click

Live since 16 October (balcony rule since 27 October)

Room Book (German: Raumbuch) is the list of every room in a building with its name, number and area that goes to the customer. WoFlV is the German living-area rule: balconies count half, space under a sloping roof counts partly, and stairs do not count.

What you would notice

  • In the Room Book's schedule tab there are two new buttons, PDF and Excel. Both carry the Lumoview logo, the address, the date and totals per flat, per level and per building, following Rio's Template 1. The PDF repeats the table header on every page and prints "Seite x von y".
  • Stairs are now deducted from the living area automatically. A room with a stair shows a "Stair excluded" badge and a note with the area without the stair. Rooms named as a staircase count as zero.
  • Balconies, loggias and terraces count half again. They had silently counted in full since an earlier change, unless an architect typed the factor by hand.
  • Totals now equal the sum of the printed rows. They used to drift by a cent or two of a square metre.
  • A room with no area says "keine Fläche erfasst" instead of showing an empty cell.

2Floor plan sheets: fewer clicks, cleaner drawings

Live since 27 October

Floor Plan Preparation is the LumoCad tab where plan sheets are made. A crop is a sheet cut around one part of a plan, for example one flat for a marketing brochure. An elevation is the drawing of a facade seen from outside. A section is a vertical cut through the building.

What you would notice

  • A new Flats button in the Views header cuts one sheet per flat on every level in one go. Before, every crop was clicked corner by corner. The cut follows the outer faces of the walls around the flat, a neighbour's wall never leaves a stub, and a flat whose rooms do not touch still gets one sheet. Regenerating keeps names and leaves hand-drawn crops alone. The sheet labels its areas "Mieteinheit".
  • The title block is readable on every sheet type. Section and elevation names no longer run over the storey cell, long project or client names wrap properly, and there is a new editable "Index" field for the revision, printed under the date.
  • Elevations no longer show a stray line between two storeys whose facades are flush. This was one of Djunejt's review points.
  • Hover or select any line in Floor Plan Preparation and a small chip tells you what it is, for example "Window · window pane seen beyond the cut". Lines on sections were hard to identify before.
  • The automatic dimension chains on facades skip door widths, as the modelling team asked.
  • Section, elevation and roof sheets no longer show a north arrow in the editor. The PDF already had none, so the editor now matches what is delivered.

3Stairs: openings follow the stair, and four ways to build one

Live since 19 October

A slab is the floor plate of a storey. A stair cuts an opening into the slab above it. Risers are the vertical steps; a winder is a wedge-shaped step in a turn.

What you would notice

  • The opening in the floor now follows the stair's shape. An L-, Z- or half-turn stair keeps the floor in its inner corners instead of cutting one big rectangle. On the plan, the void cross is drawn once per piece.
  • Each stair has a Construction setting: solid, stepped, smooth or floating, plus a thickness for the waist or treads. It shows in 3D and in the IFC download. This replaces the old view-wide solid/open toggle. It needed a release of the shared data types and the API, done the same day.
  • Changing the number of risers keeps the turns where they belong. Before, a landing could end up ten metres behind the start of the stair and the opening would cross the slab edge.
  • Smaller fixes: a custom-turn stair asks for the side click at placement like a preset does, panel values show at most a tenth of a millimetre instead of six decimals, and an opening that does not fit names the floor and says what is wrong instead of printing raw ids.

Note: on 4 November the team agreed to pause further 3D stair and roof work in favour of 2D automation. What is live stays live.

4Roofs from drone models read far more faithfully

Built, not pushed committed locally on 29 October, waiting for a go

Airteam is the drone-survey partner that delivers a 3D model of a roof as an OBJ file. LumoCad can turn such a model into an editable roof. Hosted elements are the things sitting on a roof: dormers, chimneys, skylights.

What you would notice

  • Dormers, chimneys and skylights can now sit across roof faces: a chimney on a ridge, a dormer across the knee of a mansard roof, a skylight over a hip. Before, each had to fit inside one face or it could not be placed at all. This applies to 3D, plan sheets, sections, elevations and the IFC export.
  • Airteam models are now read straight off their clean mesh instead of being flattened into a height grid first. Ridges, apexes and narrow dormers survive the import. On four test models the area error dropped from 22–40 % to 6–16 %, with no element left unplaced. Large or messy models still go through the old method, and the toast says which one ran.

5Airteam drone models arrive on their own

Built, not pushed finished 4 November, proven by 10 signed-off checks, not deployed

What you would notice once it is switched on

  • Every hour the server looks for newly delivered Airteam projects, matches each one to a building by its exact address, by a model already named after the building, or by a strong name match, and copies the model into the building's Model files. Nobody has to download and upload anything.
  • Anything unclear waits in a review list under Admin → Airteam, where an admin assigns, replaces, adds or removes the building. A decision made by a person is never overwritten by the next hourly run.
  • Once a building has a model, LumoCad and the building page show it in place of the FastFusion link, and the Airteam file opens first. A redelivered model replaces the old one cleanly.

6Smaller things people will notice

  • Live QC mails say who did it. "Anna has handed in the 3D QC check for … and assigned the corrections to you" instead of a passive sentence. Since 20 October.
  • Live QC hand-in is no longer blocked by a lagging building status. A checker with every floor checked keeps the hand-in button even if the building still says "3D postprocessing"; an amber line names the stage. Since 28 October.
  • Live Wall generation errors name the room. When a tiny knot in a room outline stops the walls from being generated, the message now names the room and storey, says what kind of knot it is, warns if it is too small to see without zooming, and says what to do. One architect had lost hours to a cryptic slab id. Since 19 October.
  • Live LumoPlan's preprocessing Team view loads faster. The database now has an index for the 45-day listing of what the annotation team touched. Since 19 October.
  • Live Security updates of the libraries the app is built on. Since 26 October.

7Groundwork: an assistant inside LumoCad

In the workshop nothing pushed, waiting on a model key

Marvin is Arne's prototype of a chat assistant that lives inside LumoCad, can look at the loaded building and propose edits that a person approves. It has a server half called Lumoagent.
  • Mapped everything Marvin can do today, about 84 actions, with a verdict for each: reuse LumoCad's own function, keep as is, replace, or drop. Arne's rule is that the assistant should call the editor's existing functions rather than carry its own copies.
  • Got the whole stack running locally: the Go service, its database and the app on Arne's branch.
  • First step done: when Marvin proposes moving a room, the move now goes through the editor's own commands, so Undo behaves exactly like a hand-made move. It has 21 automated tests but has not yet run in the real app.
  • Audited which steps of the building pipeline an agent could take over, ranked by how safe and valuable each is, and transcribed the Revit workflow videos so the agent work has the real process to read.
  • On 4 November Arne set the direction: 2D automation is the north star, 3D roof and stair work is paused, and Milton will build the agent fresh after Arne's design document.
