Port the classics; let models propose, not measure

What the literature, the libraries and the datasets offer for modelling a building from our own data: one LiDAR ring per room, panoramas at a known pose, a compass, a surveyor-recorded door graph, and sometimes a drone mesh. Four areas: sorting and puzzling rooms, walls with thickness and type, doors and windows, and the agent layer around Marvin, Calvin and the golden set.

Made from twelve research notes in two rounds (about 230 cited links). The full working document with every source is on Milton's machine at ~/Documents/lumoview/reports/Agentic building modeling research.md; the notes are in ~/Documents/lumoview/research_notes/Agentic building modeling research/. Gap ids (G1 to G28) refer to the context brief 00_lumoview_context.md in that folder.

The short version

Nothing published takes our actual input. A RotLidar "point cloud" is one horizontal ring of about 5,000 points at scanner height, rooms are scanned one at a time with no overlap, and the only cross-room evidence is the door graph. The fastest credible path in every area is a handful of small TypeScript ports of classical algorithms, each testable on Hohental within days, not the adoption of a learned model.

AREA 1 · SORTING AND PUZZLING

Exhaustive search beats random

Replace the random pose search with Cartographer's branch-and-bound matcher (Apache-2.0): 8 to 86 ms per match on a 2009 CPU, and repeatable by construction. For puzzling, the best paper gets 35 % of look-alike rooms wrong because it must guess which door matches which. Our door graph already knows.

AREA 2 · WALLS

Thickness is a registration problem

Every open pipeline measures thickness as the gap between the two faces, and none publishes a millimetre error. Our accuracy is bounded by how well rooms are puzzled. Wall type has no classifier anywhere; the precedent is "snap to the nearest catalogue width" plus position rules, as a proposal.

AREA 3 · DOORS AND WINDOWS

Measure with rays, classify with vision

Width and position should come from LiDAR geometry (gaps along the wall line, panorama mask columns intersected with the wall). Vision-language models get 45 % on the hard grounding benchmark and never say "not there". Use them only for closed questions, hosted in the EU.

AREA 4 · AGENT LAYER

Marvin's design is the right one

LLMs fully solve at most 3.4 % of BIM edits; a rule-based checker agrees with humans 94 % of the time where a vision judge gets 59 %. Keep typed tools and a human Approve, and promote the deterministic check from audit to gate. Code-as-action belongs in Calvin, behind a golden-set regression.

TWO THINGS THAT CUT ACROSS ALL FOUR

  • Licences. Most learned code and almost every dataset in this field is non-commercial (SALVe, HEAT, ZInD, Matterport3D, Structured3D, ScanNet++, CubiCasa5K) or GPL (YOLO-World, HouseDiffusion, PolyFit, CGAL kinetic partition). A second reason to port rather than adopt.
  • The scorecard gates everything. With six golden buildings of fifteen rooms, a real 10 mm improvement shows up about two times in three, and a naive per-room test manufactures wins a third of the time. Score at building level from day one.

Why ports beat adoption

Three facts decide what is usable.

1 ring

Each scanner placement gives one 2D ring (x, y, z = 0; no normals, no colour, no per-point height). Vertical structure exists only in separate "section" and "stairs" slices. Learned floor-plan networks (Floor-SP, MonteFloor, HEAT, RoomFormer, FRI-Net) all want a top-down density image of a whole floor and output polygons without thickness or openings. A dedicated search found no learned method that takes one room's ring and returns a polygon. Our own line-fit plus arrangement (v1, v2) is, for this input, the state of the art.

0 overlap

Rooms are scanned one at a time. The 3D registration toolkits (TEASER++, small_gicp, KISS-ICP, Open3D, GTSAM) need feature correspondences or substantial overlap, are C++ or Python, and have no maintained browser build (the one WebAssembly port, pcl.js, has been archived since 2023). The cross-room evidence we do have is the door-frame scan type and the capture app's door graph (doorLeadsTo, spaceConnections), which no calculation uses today (G4).

TS + EU

All geometry runs in the architect's browser tab in TypeScript, or could run in a Node worker; nothing runs headless (G25). Customer data stays in the EU (Scaleway, Paris). The product is commercial, so MIT/BSD/Apache is preferred and GPL may only run as a separate server process. "Directly usable" therefore means TypeScript, or portable in a few hundred lines. That is exactly the size of the algorithms that matter here.

Area 1 · Sorting and puzzling

Branch-and-bound replaces the random search; the door graph replaces the integer program.

A deterministic single-room pose search G1

Today alignScansWithGridCorrelation rasterises each cloud onto a 100 mm grid and maximises overlap with Differential Evolution, a population-based random search: 5,050 evaluations, 5 to 10 s per pair, not repeatable unless seeded. Robotics settled this fifteen years ago with correlative scan matching: evaluate every pose in a window, using a coarse grid whose cells hold the maximum of the fine cells they cover, so coarse scores are provable upper bounds and whole regions can be discarded without missing the optimum. Olson 2009 measured 8.4 ms (0.5 m / 20° window), 20.8 ms (2 m / 40°) and 86.1 ms (4 m / 90°) on one 2009 CPU thread, against 65 s for the naive scan. Hess 2016 generalised it into Cartographer's depth-first branch-and-bound with max-pooled grids of 1, 4, 16 and 64 cells; the FastCorrelativeScanMatcher2D source is Apache-2.0 and is the model to port.

For us (inference): 300 to 600 lines of TypeScript inside the existing worker, 4 to 6 days, low risk; a 2 m prior window costs a few milliseconds with branch-and-bound. It replaces the random search inside multiScanGridAlignment and the door-frame fitting, but not room-to-room placement, which needs a different objective. Open utils/alignment/deprecated/csmAlignment.ts first: it may be a half-finished correlative matcher. FFT phase correlation (MIT libraries fft.js, webfft, kissfft-wasm) is a cheaper seed for same-room placements, with a four-fold rotation ambiguity on right-angled rooms.

Puzzling rooms with the surveyor's door graph G4

Three published lines come close, and all three are instructive mainly for where they fail. Floorplan-Jigsaw (ICCV 2019) reaches 85 % global placement on 101 mostly synthetic scenes but assumes one closed ring of Manhattan fragments and never released code. SALVe (Zillow, 2022) proposes door-to-door alignments from panoramas, verifies them with a learned network and solves a pose graph, localising 81 % of panoramas; its licence is CC BY-NC-ND, so only the ideas can be reused. The closest is Block Assembly (Yan, Nan, Wonka, SIGGRAPH Asia 2016): it detects door "portals" in isolated room scans, aligns two scans from a portal pair by shifting one room by the wall thickness along the door normal, and chooses which pairs to believe with an integer program under a no-penetration constraint. On a 102-room synthetic floor 35 % of rooms landed in the wrong place because identical-looking rooms are interchangeable, and conflict enumeration took over four hours at 156 scans.

The lesson. The hard part of every published method is the discrete question which door matches which, and we already have the answer in doorLeadsTo. No system was found that takes a measured door graph plus real per-room scans. So the formulation is an own design (inference): one pose per room; for every door-graph edge a term that makes the two door midpoints coincide after a shift of the wall thickness (75 to 800 mm) with opposite normals; a non-overlap penalty with Clipper (already a dependency); a weak compass prior plus a snap to the storey's dominant directions; and a late exterior term. Initialise from a spanning tree of the door graph, refine with Levenberg-Marquardt. The dense pose-graph solver in pointToLineGlobalICP.ts already handles 90 unknowns. Treat door-frame scans as trusted edges and door-graph-only edges as uncertain ones, as Open3D's multiway registration does. 8 to 12 days, medium risk. Marvin's own 4,266-line registration stack produced 0 safe candidates on Gewobag OG01/OG04: the measured cost of attacking this without the door graph.

Splits, merges, storey stacking, compass G7 G5 G6

Room segmentation on an occupancy grid is classical: Bormann 2016 measured the Voronoi method at 95.0 % recall / 94.8 % precision on 20 maps; the Fraunhofer package is non-commercial, but the morphological and distance variants are about a hundred lines each, so reimplement (3 to 5 days). Storey stacking has almost no literature; the design is a per-section histogram of horizontal edges for slab levels, chained through section or stair scans that span two storeys, with XY alignment by minimising the symmetric difference of the exterior outline and the stair core, which is what the Revit architects do by hand (6 to 8 days, medium-high risk, no benchmark). Compass: createImuNorthPrediction applies no declination; WMM2025 is public domain (npm geomagnetism, Apache-2.0) but needs a geocoded address, and the one indoor study found 12.45° average heading error for phones. Treat the compass as a ±15° prior after snapping and as a per-storey consistency check (1 to 2 days).

Area 2 · Walls

Thickness is a registration problem; type is a catalogue snap; exterior thickness without a drone model has a free consistency check but no measurement.

Choosing between outline hypotheses objectively G8 G10

v1 versus v2 is decided by taste, Marvin defaults to v1 without reference directions (editorOps/service.ts:474), and the per-room confidence is hidden. The ISPRS Indoor Modelling benchmark supplies a ready definition without one-to-one matching: completeness (share of the reference within a buffer of the model), correctness (share of the model within the buffer of the reference) and accuracy (median distance), reported as curves over 1 to 15 cm; its teams reach 0.5 to 6.7 cm. Ported to a single room (inference): completeness = share of cloud points within the buffer of the outline, correctness = share of outline length with point support (Marvin's existing measuredEdgeSupport rule: 30 mm corridor, 100 mm bins, coverage ≥ 0.7), accuracy = RMS point-to-segment distance, plus a visibility test (the scanner origin must lie inside and no ray may cross the polygon) and a vertex-count penalty so the convex-hull fallback cannot win. Compute for v1, v2 and the hull, pick the best per room, show it as the confidence. 2 to 3 days. Write it once as a pure function that Marvin, Calvin and the scorecard share.

Thickness: every pipeline pairs the two faces, nobody reports an error G12 G13

Cloud2BIM (MIT, Python, CPU) slices a density histogram, pairs opposite parallel wall surfaces, adds a virtual face where the outer side is unscanned, and bounds thickness by min/max parameters; it found 24 of 25 zones with deviations up to 25 mm and, by its authors' own words, reports no thickness error because "no uniform benchmark exists". Ochmann 2019 does the same pairing inside a 3D cell complex solved with Gurobi and sometimes splits one thick wall into two thin ones; Bassier gives walls seen from one side a 100 mm default. For us (inference): the two faces of an interior wall come from two rooms' rings, so thickness equals the gap between the puzzled inner faces, which boundaryWallThicknessAnalysis.ts already measures, and its accuracy is bounded by the puzzle registration (100 mm grid in sorting, 50 mm in fine-tune), not by the estimator. Improving thickness means improving area 1. The generator's blind spot (walls never look at the cloud) is best closed by running the cloud-support score on every wall face before acceptance; auditDesiredCloudWalls already does it after the fact.

Type: "snap to the nearest catalogue width" G14

No published rule set or classifier infers masonry versus drywall or load-bearing from scans. The one precedent is Bassier 2019: snap the measured width to the user's catalogue, argmin |d_w − d_r|. The German catalogue follows DIN 4172 (12.5 cm module minus a 1 cm joint: 11.5, 24, 36.5 cm, with 17.5 and 30 cm as standard widths). Gründerzeit buildings need a second catalogue: the Vienna rules run in 0.15 m steps (0.30, 0.45, 0.60, 0.75 m), thickening by one step per storey downwards; the North German 38 / 51 / 64 cm series is plausible but has no source. The drywall list (75, 100, 125, 150 mm) is unverified against any Knauf or Rigips sheet. A measured face-to-face distance includes plaster on both sides, so the rule should propose, not auto-apply, unless neighbouring walls agree. Position gives the rest: on the slab outline = exterior; ≥ 30 cm exterior = masonry; 17.5 to 24 cm interior = likely load-bearing; ≤ 12.5 cm = ambiguous; vertical continuity across storeys raises load-bearing probability; the capture app's door-reveal Thickness is an independent measurement per opening. 2 days as a Wall Type Organizer proposer.

Exterior thickness without drone model or outside scan G12

About 20 % of buildings have neither; today every exterior wall is the 40 cm slab offset. Three designs, all inference. Airteam mesh: thickness per facade is the offset between the interior wall line and the nearest facade plane (roofMesh.ts already fits planes), never the eave, because roof outlines include overhangs; registration literature only claims "promising" results, so expect 5 to 20 cm lateral error. Reveal depth: a door-frame scan or a scan through an open window gives both jamb faces; one study matched tape within ±6 mm, but glass produces ghost points. Cadastre: the NRW ALKIS buildings are live and free: WFS wfs_nw_alkis_vereinfacht, feature type ave:GebaeudeBauwerk, licence "Datenlizenz Deutschland – Zero" (verified from GetCapabilities), one GeoPackage per Kreis (Cologne 177 MB), LoD2 roofs from ogc-api.nrw.de. No text says whether plaster or insulation is inside the line, and no NRW accuracy class was found (Bavaria demands 3 cm for clear corners). So the footprint fixes orientation and the outer outline to roughly ±10 cm plus an unknown render offset: enough to flag a facade at 24 or 55 cm instead of 40, not enough to set 36.5 versus 42.5. OpenStreetMap is not an alternative: about 4 m average offset against official footprints in Munich; the ~/Documents/lumoview/osm experiments carry that error.

Slanted walls and joints G9 G15 G16

Cheapest step for slanted walls: a length-weighted mean-shift over all rawEdges directions per building, proposing every well-supported cluster beyond the dominant orthogonal pair as a reference plane; generateBuildingMainGuidelines() already clusters directions and is unused (1 to 2 days). A "2D kinetic partition" (wall segments grow as rays that stop at their first or second hit; the planar analogue of CGAL's GPL 3D version) would cut the spurious cells of v1's full arrangement, 300 to 600 lines, untested. For joints and generator robustness the literature has nothing; the one thing to port is the ISPRS score as the acceptance gate for regenerated walls, so "cannot create an outer wall loop" becomes a measured regression. All geometry packages involved are permissive and small (clipper2-js Boost, polygon-clipping MIT, @flatten-js/core MIT, robust-predicates Unlicense, delaunator / earcut ISC); the platform already ships clipper-lib.

Area 3 · Doors and windows

Measure with LiDAR rays, map the panorama masks we already have onto the wall, classify with EU-hosted vision models, and never let a model be the measuring instrument.

Width and position come from the ring, not the picture G17

The classic scan-based detectors (Adan and Huber 2011, Xiong 2013) label every wall cell as occupied, occluded or empty by ray tracing from the scanner position. Collapsed to one dimension for a ring (inference): along each rawEdges wall line, a run of azimuths where the ray passes through the wall into the next room is an open doorway, and its width follows from the two edge intersections. Limits are physical: a closed door is a flush plane and invisible to the scan; windows return hits or nothing depending on the glass; sills and lintels need a section scan or the panorama. Door-frame scans see both rooms and are the best cross-room evidence. 2 to 4 days, scored against capture widths and the approved openings in the hand-in backups.

Map the existing panorama masks onto the wall before training anything

Every panorama already has server-side masks for door, window, floor, ceiling and radiator (objectTags, 2048 × 4096), today "a visualisation only". Nothing in the literature maps a panorama detection to a wall position, so the geometry is stated as inference: a mask's left and right columns give azimuths, corrected by the measurement rotation and the device offset; cast each ray from the scanner position and intersect it with the wall line; the two hits give position and width in millimetres, the vertical extent gives sill and head. The error budget is dominated by yaw, not pixels: one pixel is 4.6 mm at 3 m, while a 0.5° yaw error adds 26 mm at 3 m, and grazing incidence multiplies errors about six-fold. Expect 1 to 3 cm width error with good yaw; fuse several panoramas by least squares. Deduplicate the same door seen from both rooms with doorLeadsTo as the pairing prior; a Hungarian assignment matches detections to the hand-measured capture openings, which Marvin's openingPlacement/capture.ts can then place. 3 to 5 days, medium risk because nobody has measured the mask quality. This is the audit's "openings proposer" without any new model.

Detectors and vision-language models

If the masks prove weak, the open-vocabulary detectors with usable licences are Grounding DINO 1.0 (Apache-2.0, 52.5 AP zero-shot on COCO), OWLv2 (Apache-2.0) and Florence-2 (MIT); RT-DETR (Apache-2.0) is the fine-tuning target that avoids Ultralytics' AGPL. No paper reports door or window accuracy for any of them on indoor images; the nearest numbers are exterior facades (F1 0.85). The commercial path is auto-labelling about 300 perspective crops of our own panoramas, hand-correcting, and fine-tuning RT-DETR on an EU GPU. Browser deployment is a non-starter (Grounding DINO tiny is 151 to 719 MB); panoramas are uploaded anyway, so detection stays server-side.

Vision-language models classify; they must never measure. On the hard GroundingME benchmark the best open model (Qwen3-VL-235B) reaches 45.1 %, Gemini-2.5-Pro 20.7 %, and the rejection score is 0.0: the models draw a box even when the object is absent. Counting doors and windows on floor plans plateaus at 0.40 to 0.55 exact match (AECV-Bench, search summary). No source reports accuracy for door width, sill height, swing side or glazing. The role that survives: closed questions on a crop that already contains one opening (door or window, hinge side, glazing, side light, archway) with an explicit "none" option and a human confirm, tested first on 100 labelled doors from approved models.

EU hosting, ranked by legal simplicity: Scaleway Generative APIs in Paris (zero data retention by default, Apache-2.0 vision models Qwen3-VL and Mistral Small 3.2; prices unverified) > Mistral's EU endpoint (10 % upcharge, zero retention on request) > AWS Bedrock EU cross-region profiles (verify per model) and Azure Data Zone > OpenRouter, Marvin's current route, which keeps prompts in-region only on Business and Enterprise plans. Marvin as configured is not EU-pinned; that is a decision for Milton, not a default.

Consistency, swing, semantics G18 G19

The 88.5 / 85.5 / 84.5 cm window spacing is a layout-regularisation problem with a solved formulation (Jiang, Nan et al. 2016: detect alignment, equal-size and equal-spacing constraints, then move each element minimally). In one dimension: cluster openings of a wall by width and type, fit a pitch and offset, snap a centre only if its residual is below about 3 cm and never above 5 cm, otherwise flag; share the along-facade coordinate across storeys. About 100 lines, propose rather than apply. Swing side has no benchmark anywhere; the ring helps: an open leaf appears as a short segment nearly perpendicular to the wall ending at the hinge. For closed doors, a vision question plus "swings into the smaller room" are low-confidence proposals. The semantics are already modelled (NOTDEFINED = deliberate open doorway; the OpeningFeatures pset); what is missing is the client symbol sign-off, which no research supplies.

Area 4 · The agent layer: Marvin and Calvin

Deterministic checkers gate, typed tools write, code stays in Calvin.

What LLMs actually do to geometry G20

The benchmarks all point the same way. BIM-Edit (324 natural-language edits on IFC models): the best model averages 49.5 % and no model fully solves more than 3.4 % of tasks. BlenderGym: humans beat every model 3 to 30× on every metric, open models fail to produce a runnable script in over 75 % of geometry edits beyond 80 lines, and vision verifiers agree with human judgments 0.66 at best against 0.79 human-to-human. SceneCritic: a rule-based layout checker agrees with humans 94 % versus 59 % for a vision judge, which also flips its ranking with the camera viewpoint. The two published BIM authoring agents (Text2BIM, MCP4IFC) both put a deterministic checker in the loop. Our readiness report, which passes when slab minus rooms equals walls within 1 mm² while pointCloudAgreement.verified has never been true, is exactly the failure CAD-bench warns about: "an artifact can compile and render yet be wrong".

The practical reading. Marvin's design (typed propose tools, a human Approve on every card, writes through editor events in one undo batch, never running code) is the one the evidence supports. The missing piece: the deterministic checker (cloud-support, origin containment, the ISPRS score) must run on every proposal before the card is shown and annotate it ("edge 3 unsupported, 41 % coverage"), with failing edges returned to the model as the repair prompt, as Text2BIM does. The one attempt at an LLM drawing an outline from a rendered cloud image ("Flur 32") was rejected, consistent with all of the above.

Tool design for 84 tools and an 81,331-character prompt G27

Anthropic's Tool Search (definitions loaded on demand) moved Opus 4 from 49 % to 74 % on an internal MCP evaluation with about 85 % fewer tokens, recommended above ten tools or 10k tokens of definitions; whether it exists through OpenRouter for non-Anthropic models is unverified. On prompt length, IFScale shows the best frontier model following about 68 % of 500 simultaneous instructions, and SCALEDIF attributes much of the loss to conflicts between instructions; Marvin's prompt is known to contain contradictions, so resolve those before shortening. Session memory should stay structured (proposal ids, approved or rejected with reasons, entities touched), not free-text summaries: AMA-Bench finds many memory designs underperform plain long context. Splitting Marvin into several LLM agents is not supported: Anthropic's own multi-agent gains come with the statement that domains needing shared context "are not a good fit", and the MAST taxonomy attributes 44 % of multi-agent failures to system design. One proposer, deterministic checker tools, and at most a read-subagent that summarises heavy point-cloud context.

Approval telemetry and Calvin

Acceptance rate alone is a weak signal (Copilot: 27 % acceptance predicted perceived productivity; Jellyfish argues the metric lost meaning once agents edit many lines). Marvin should log per card: shown, approved unchanged, approved then edited within N minutes (traceable through the undo batch id), rejected with a one-tap reason (today rejections carry none), and time to decision. That is the "human edits after the pass" number of the scorecard. For Calvin, the measured case for code-as-action is real but conditional: SWE-agent's interface raised GPT-4 by 64 % relative, with lint-on-edit, a dedicated edit command and a short observation window each worth points; BlenderGym's lesson is that long hand-wired scripts defeat most models. The design: a reviewed lab-module registry where a module is promoted to a tool only after a golden-set regression run, Calvin's G1/G4 scores plus the outline score as the feedback after each edit. The demo's floor-sorting algorithm written "in about a minute" that "may be overfitted" is precisely what that gate exists to catch.

Golden set and scorecard: measure at building level or measure nothing

The scorecard is the first deliverable, not the last: its outline and wall score is the same function that gates Marvin's cards.

WHICH METRICS, IN MILLIMETRES

  • The learned-floor-plan papers (MonteFloor, HEAT, RoomFormer) match rooms by IoU and count corners within 10 pixels on 256 × 256 images. Nothing in millimetres. Transfer the geometric protocols instead: the ISPRS completeness / correctness / accuracy triple with buffer curves, and the USIBD RealityMapping2FloorPlan challenge (room IoU, endpoint precision/recall at several distances, wall-orientation similarity, topology errors).
  • USIBD Level of Accuracy, at 95 % confidence (two independent academic quotations; spec itself behind an account): LOA30 = 5 to 15 mm, LOA20 = 15 to 50 mm, LOA10 = 50 to 150 mm. Our 30 mm corridor straddles LOA20 and LOA30, so report a 95th percentile of edge error, not a mean. At 1:100 a 0.25 mm pen line is 25 mm: 30 mm is a defensible bar by arithmetic alone.
  • Scorecard v1 (inference): outline agreement per room (one-to-one Hungarian match at IoU ≥ 0.5, corner recall at 30/50/100 mm, Chamfer and 95th-percentile Hausdorff, split/merge counts for G7); per-edge cloud support as a distribution with "explained gaps" and a signed median offset; walls by the ISPRS triple plus thickness error and type match; openings in wall coordinates plus door-graph precision/recall against spaceConnections; sorting as pose error over five seeds; areas as DIN 277 and WoFlV deltas; human effort as an HTER-style edit count per element plus the untouched share.
  • Two conventions to fix first: inner wall face or centre line as ground truth, and which area rule each customer uses. WoFlV §3 and §4 were read from the law (clear dimensions from the cladding; 1 to 2 m height counts half; balconies "in der Regel zu einem Viertel, höchstens jedoch zur Hälfte").

ROOMS ARE NOT INDEPENDENT

  • Rooms in one building share the builder, the scanner session and the walls. A researcher's own Monte Carlo (600 runs per cell, not a published result): six buildings of fifteen rooms, a true 10 mm improvement, between-building spread 10 mm and within-building 20 mm. A cluster-bootstrap lower bound detects the gain with power 0.67; an exact sign-flip permutation on six building means only 0.27; a naive t-test over 90 rooms gives 31 % false positives at a nominal 5 %. More rooms per building barely help; more buildings do.
  • Operating rule: report effect sizes with building-level intervals and "improved in k of 6 buildings" (k = 6 is p = 0.031 under a sign test), estimate the spreads from the first two scored buildings and re-plan, aim for 10 to 12 buildings over time, McNemar for binary outcomes such as "needed an edit".
  • Tiers: gold (hand-verified, frozen, never tuned on; Hohental first, then a building with slanted walls and a corridor-heavy floor, growing to 6 to 8 stratified by age, construction, evidence available, use, roof), silver (the hand-in backups: scans, boundaries and observations but no walls or doors, so sorting and outlines only), synthetic (a TypeScript ray-caster with exact ground truth, calibrated on a flat wall from a real scan).
  • Practice: a fixed split file at building level, a noise floor from a second architect re-tracing 20 % of one building, annotation inside LumoCad itself, DVC (Apache-2.0) to a Scaleway bucket or Git LFS for the clouds, and the golden version, tool commit and random seed stored in every scorecard output.

The surprise of this survey is how little of the field's recent progress applies and how much of its older, smaller work does. A 2009 scan matcher, a 2016 portal-assembly paper, a 2019 catalogue-snap rule and a 2018 benchmark metric each solve one of our gaps almost verbatim, in a few hundred lines of TypeScript, and the door graph the capture app has recorded all along removes the one combinatorial problem the best of those papers could not. Our data, not any model, is the edge.
