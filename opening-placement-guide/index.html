LumoCad · Opening review · architect-facing guidance

Every proposed opening comes with a claim about how sure we are.

The opening-confidence ladder turns panorama evidence, building geometry and optional human marks into a position an architect can use—or an honest explanation of what is still unknown. It reveals useful partial facts without disguising them as certainty.

Project Lumoview-Building-Analytics-GmbH/platformBuild 8314ef6b9Updated 10 November 2026

The practical idea. A survey record knows a door or window’s kind, dimensions and connected rooms. A panorama mask or human frame mark knows where it was seen. LumoCad combines those facts with walls and room outlines, then labels the result from Exact to Wall unknown. The architect can act on a rough position while seeing exactly what must be checked before saving.

The safe boundary

The guide can focus views, show a blue preview and prepare an attach dialog. It never silently creates geometry.

Only the existing native Attach Door or Attach Window dialog saves. Cancel leaves the model unchanged; native Undo remains the undo path.

From evidence to an architect’s next move

One shared confidence function drives the grader and guide.

1 · Read facts

Capture + plan

Each record carries kind, width, height, sill and linked rooms.

  • Walls and room outlines
  • Aligned panorama scans
  • Existing openings

2 · Find evidence

Masks + marks

AI masks and optional human frame marks both become camera rays projected onto candidate walls.

  • Mask edges
  • Manual fixed sides
  • Independent scan views

3 · Reconcile

Fit + assess

The engine matches evidence to one capture record, tests width and room adjacency, and measures agreement.

  • Exclusive evidence ownership
  • Wall and placement safety
  • Confidence band

4 · Facilitate

Show the right help

The guide orders reliable items first, labels every claim and gives one concrete next action when a placement is not safe.

Why the ladder matters: a rough position on the correct wall is still useful. It is shown as a rough position, never promoted to an Exact claim. When the wall itself is unknown, the UI deliberately shows no invented blue aperture.

What evidence counts

AI panorama masks

A mask’s left and right edges become rays from the scan camera. A fit must meet a plausible wall between the captured rooms and match the recorded dimensions.

  • Only aligned panoramas count
  • Complete masks can support a candidate
  • Partial masks ≥35% can anchor a rough wall position

Human door/window marks

Two manual fixed-frame sides are placement evidence, not an override that hides masks. They use the same panorama-to-wall geometry and may corroborate or contradict AI evidence.

  • Existing observations are preserved
  • Exterior marks use the room-facing wall face
  • One mark is useful but normally only Close

Plan constraints

Walls, room outlines and occupied openings keep a visually plausible mask from becoming a structurally implausible placement.

  • Correct linked rooms at the fit
  • Fits within a wall; does not overlap
  • Missing outline, size or alignment is explicit

The confidence ladder

These are claims, not decoration. The guide shows the badge and its evidence reason. Stronger evidence can raise a claim; disagreement always lowers it.

Why “Exact” is not survey-grade truth

Exact is a bounded interaction claim: available evidence agrees tightly enough to put the opening within ±50 mm of its suggested centre. It does not measure construction tolerances, swing direction, sill/height accuracy, or whether the architect’s reference is a survey.

How an architect uses the guide

  1. Open Review openings. The guide collects captured records and photo detections on the active level. Unlinked detections remain separate; they are not silently converted into openings.
  2. Start with the strongest evidence. Positions are ordered Exact → Close → On this wall. Each has a D/W tag, dimensions, linked rooms, confidence badge and plain-language reason.
  3. Inspect in both views. Show proposed position focuses Top View and the supporting panorama. A blue preview appears only when a position exists.
  4. Review or correct. Open the native attach dialog to save, or choose Adjust frame and mark the two fixed jambs. Supporting panoramas are available when there is more than one.
  5. Resolve uncertainty in place. For Wall unknown and No position, Show in both views goes to the relevant photo and wall without pretending a placement exists.

Beyond a binary detector

A binary detector either draws a door or stays silent. The guide exposes the useful middle ground:

It shows a rough wall position when a partial mask is enough to orient an architect, with an On this wall badge and a direct instruction to move it to the jambs.

It does not draw a confident-looking aperture when several walls compete, a record is ambiguous, or required geometry is missing.

Strict versus exploratory

The general review has strict, balanced and exploratory suggestion levels. Strict does not reveal a 39%-visible partial opening as ready. The beginner guide intentionally uses exploratory mode so useful rough evidence is visible—but its lower confidence is explicit.

Safeguards that prevent false certainty

Evidence ownership

A complete mask belongs to one best matching capture record. A partial mask may give a larger same-room record a diagnostic wall hint, but cannot steal a complete detection. If one detection fits multiple records, the guide asks the architect to choose.

Wide unmarked doors

A door wider than 2.5 m with mask evidence only is not automatically placed. It becomes a wall-level review item until a human mark confirms its frame. This avoids a confident-looking but 720 mm misplaced automatic placement.

Safety before saving

Before the attach workflow is offered, candidates are checked against wall bounds, occupied openings, room relationships and current model revision. Locked rooms are named and may be deliberately unlocked; the guide never bypasses the editor lock.

Manual correction is evidence

Two fixed-frame clicks create a candidate that is rechecked through the same geometry and safety path as a mask proposal. A failed mark remains an explanation, never a partly saved object.

How to test

In LumoCad

  1. Open a level with captured openings, aligned panoramas and room outlines, then start Review openings.
  2. Choose an Exact item. Check its badge, reason, blue preview and supporting view in Top View and panorama.
  3. Choose an On this wall item. Confirm it says to move to jambs before saving; strict review must not present 39%-visible evidence as ready.
  4. Choose Wall unknown. Confirm no blue aperture appears, but Show in both views reaches the relevant photo and location.
  5. Mark two fixed sides. Confirm it becomes reviewable only after wall and room checks pass.
  6. Open an attach dialog and press Cancel. Confirm no opening was created.

What this does not measure

Unknown or boundaryWhy it matters
Architect comprehension and time savedThe labels are designed for architects, but this build does not measure understanding or editing time in a live project.
Guide-path calibration on production dataThe automated grader measures the engine path using the same ladder. The review UI has focused tests, but not a separate real-building calibration grade.
Reference accuracyThe Hohental reference is an architect’s placed opening, not survey-grade truth.
Missing or weak source dataBuildings without object tags, panorama yaw alignment or human marks can still be reviewed, but more often end at Wall unknown or No position.
Properties beyond locationThe ladder does not verify swing side, facing, partitioning, sill/height accuracy or construction detail.
Glossary
  • Captured opening: a door/window site record with dimensions and connected rooms, but not necessarily a placed aperture.
  • Mask: an AI segmentation outline in a 360 panorama.
  • Human mark: a manual record of two fixed frame sides in a panorama.
  • Wall fit: where evidence rays intersect a wall whose rooms and observed width match the record.
  • Candidate: a proposed position; it becomes building geometry only after native attach.
