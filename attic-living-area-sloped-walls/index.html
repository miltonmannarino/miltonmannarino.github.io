Attic living area without a roof model

An attic room counts less living area where its ceiling slopes down. LumoCad used to need a full 3D roof to know that. Now the architect tells the room which walls the ceiling slopes down to, how high it starts and how steep it is, and LumoCad works out the rest.

Why this exists

German living area (WoFlV, the Wohnflächenverordnung) counts a room's floor by how much headroom it has. The height is measured straight up from the floor to the ceiling:

2 m or morecounts in full
1 m to under 2 mcounts half
under 1 mdoes not count

Until now LumoCad got those heights from a modelled 3D roof. Since the switch to a 2D-only workflow (4 Nov 2026) roofs are not modelled any more, yet clients still pay for the living-area figure. In Revit the architects do it by hand: cut a section, find the 1 m and 2 m lines, project them onto the plan, hatch each zone. This feature does that cut for them.

How it works

  1. Mark the sloped walls. In the room panel, switch on each wall the ceiling slopes down to. Usually that is the façade wall under the roof.
  2. Give each one two numbers. The knee height: how high the ceiling is where it meets that wall (the low wall under the slope, “Drempel”). The pitch: how steep the roof is, in degrees.
  3. LumoCad draws the ceiling. From the wall the ceiling rises at the pitch until it reaches the room's normal flat ceiling height, and stays flat from there.
  4. The 1 m and 2 m lines fall out. The 1 m line runs parallel to the wall at a distance of (1 m − knee) ÷ tan(pitch); the 2 m line the same with 2 m. Between them the floor counts half, in front of the 1 m line nothing.

Four rules that make it work on real rooms

  • A slope only counts in front of its own wall. A room behind a roof loggia can slope left and right of it and stay flat in the middle.
  • Where two slopes meet, the lower one wins. That gives the ridge of a gable room and the corner of a hipped room without drawing either.
  • The slope line does not have to be one of the room's own walls. A small WC standing 2 m in from the façade still lies under the façade's slope. Give it the façade line and it gets the right height.
  • What a person typed still wins. A hand-entered WoFlV area or factor beats the slopes; the room's own slopes beat a modelled roof; with neither, nothing changes.

Does it match the architects?

Checked against a real delivered room book: the attic flat of Fürstenwalder Poststraße 88, Frankfurt (Oder), where the architects worked out WoFlV in Revit. Pitch 37° as measured from the vertical scans (the architects' cross-section drawing says about 37,7°), knee height 0, the rooms' outlines taken to the façade walls.

The living room of that flat counts 94 % in the room book, and the scans show why: most of it sits under a wide dormer, so only the few truly sloped wall pieces should be marked. The neighbouring building (Nr. 91–93) has six more identical flats with the same percentages.

One surprise from the check. In both Frankfurt buildings the sloped rooms were drawn in LumoCad 35–55 % too small: the outline stops where the scanner's beam hit the slope instead of at the wall. That makes even the plain floor area (DIN 277) wrong. The numbers above use the outlines taken to the walls; fixing short outlines is on the list below.

What changed

Room panel

  • New list “Sloped walls” under “Ceiling slope”: one switch per wall of the room, with its length.
  • A switched-on wall shows Knee height (m) and Pitch (°). A new wall starts with the room's surveyed ceiling slope, or the last values used.
  • A slope line that no longer sits on a wall (the outline was moved, or a façade line) is listed with Remove.
  • “WoFlV from sloped walls” shows the living area and the < 1 m / 1–2 m split live.

Floor plan and room book

  • The printed WoFlV figure, the coloured area zones (sheet option “Show WoFlV area zones”) and the room book use the room's slopes.
  • Plan warnings say where the figure came from: “from its sloped walls” or “from the roof above it”.

Saving and undo

  • The slopes are saved on the room, load with the building and come back with Ctrl+Z like any other room edit.
  • Editing another room field never clears them.

For developers

  • Geometry: src/lumocad/utils/floorplan/roomCeiling.ts. It turns the slopes into ceiling triangles, the same shape a roof's underside has, so the existing WoFlV code needs no special case.
  • Room property ceilingSlopes, event space.setCeilingSlopes.
  • Panel: RoomSlopedWallsEditor.tsx.

How to test

In the app (not done yet)

  1. Copy the Frankfurt (Oder) building, Fürstenwalder Poststraße 88–90, from prod to dev and open its attic floor (Floor 4) in LumoCad.
  2. Select the room “DG04 M Zimmer1”. Fix its outline first: drag the façade-side edge out to the façade wall, since it was drawn too short.
  3. In the right panel under Sloped walls, switch on the façade wall. Set Knee height 0 and Pitch 37.
  4. Expect “WoFlV from sloped walls” close to the room book's 13,59 m². Turn on Show WoFlV area zones in the floor plan: red, yellow and green strips along the façade.
  5. Press Ctrl+Z: the slope and the figure go back. Reload the page: a saved slope is still there.
