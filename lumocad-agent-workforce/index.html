LumoCad · production pipeline · audit of 27 October 2026 · plain-language edition

Where an "agent workforce" could take over work between scan and floor plan

Who this is for. Anyone at Lumoview. It assumes no knowledge of LumoCad, of software, or of how the production pipeline works. Every technical word is explained the first time it appears and again in the glossary at the end. Short notes in monospace are pointers for developers; everyone else can skip them.

The question Milton asked. Our pipeline turns a scanned building into a floor plan and a room book. Which of its steps could an "agent" (a piece of software that uses artificial intelligence to do a task and hands the result to a person for review) do for us, or mostly do for us, so that people only check instead of doing?

How this was checked. I read the LumoCad manuals and code, the code of the server ("the API") and of LumoPlan, the company's existing specification for running agents, the Notion pages that describe the Revit workflow and the Q3/Q4 goals, and the Revit production workflow guide (a written version of how the architects build the model in Revit today). Where a claim comes from code, I say so.

1 · Start here: how a building becomes a floor plan

Think of one building travelling through seven stations. At each station either a person or the software does something to it.

Measure→1 Sort→2 Puzzle→3 Boundaries→hand-in→4 Walls→5 Openings→3D QC→6 Floor plan→2D QC→7 Room book→delivery
  1. Station 0

    Measurement, on site

    A person walks through the building with the Lumoview scanner and puts it down one or more times in every room. Each time it records three things:

    • A point cloud. A laser spins and measures the distance to everything around it thousands of times per second. The result is a cloud of dots; every dot is one spot on a wall, floor, window or piece of furniture, with its position in space. A point cloud is not a drawing. It is raw dots.
    • A panorama: a 360° photo taken from the same spot.
    • Motion-sensor data (the "IMU"): how much the scanner was tilted and roughly which way was north.

    Doors and windows are measured by hand with a measuring tool and typed into the capture app: width, height, and which two rooms the door connects. We call these the capture openings. Everything uploads to the Lumoview server.

  2. Station 1

    Sorting: scans → rooms

    Each scanner placement is one measurement. One room usually has several measurements, taken from different spots. They do not know where they are relative to each other. "Sorting" means two things: (1) put every measurement into the right room (a measurement in no room is called unsorted); (2) rotate and slide the measurements of one room until their dots overlap and together draw the room's outline.

    Today the software does step 2 automatically with the room solver: it tries thousands of positions and keeps the one where the dots overlap best. This "how well do the dots overlap" number is called a correlation score (the developers use two of them, NCC and IoU; both just mean "overlap from 0 to 1"). A person starts the solver, looks at every room, and fixes what went wrong with hand tools.

  3. Station 2

    Puzzle: rooms → floor → building

    After station 1 every room has a shape, but the rooms float independently on the screen, spread out on a grid. A person drags each room into place next to its neighbours, like puzzle pieces, until the floor looks like the real floor. Then the floors are stacked on top of each other at the right heights (the storey elevation is the height of a floor above the ground).

  4. Station 3

    Boundaries: dots → room outline

    Dots are not a drawing. For each room the software (or a person) draws a clean polygon along the inside face of the walls. This polygon is the room's boundary. From it come the room's area and, later, the walls.

  5. Station 4

    Walls

    Where two neighbouring room boundaries run parallel with a gap between them, the gap is a wall. The software turns these gaps into wall objects with a thickness. A person then decides each wall's type (for example "24 cm masonry" or "10 cm drywall"); the type is what the client and Revit need.

  6. Station 5

    Doors, windows, stairs

    On the panoramas a person marks each door and window by clicking its four corners. Such a mark is called an observation. Each observation then has to be matched with the hand-measured capture opening from station 0 (so the door gets its real width and height), and the door has to be placed into the right wall at the right spot. Stairs are placed by hand with a few clicks and typed values.

  7. Station 6

    Floor plan (2D)

    From the 3D model the software draws the 2D plan: walls, doors with their swing arcs, windows, room names and areas, dimension lines, section drawings (vertical cuts through the building), and sheets with a title block. It exports a PDF (to look at) and a DXF (a CAD file that architects can edit). Today many clients still receive plans produced in Revit (Autodesk's modelling program, which the architects at Lumoview use), not from LumoCad.

  8. Station 7

    Room book (Raumbuch)

    A table of all rooms: name, number, areas, and the properties typed into the checklist (flooring, wall finish, and so on). Areas come in several legal definitions: DIN 277 (the standard way to measure a room's floor area), WoFlV (the German living-area rule, which for example counts a balcony at 50 % and a cellar at 0 %), and BGF (gross floor area of the whole floor including walls). Delivered as Excel and PDF.

  9. Around it

    Quality control and the process

    After stations 1–5 (3D) and after station 6 (2D) a checker goes through a checklist and a corrector fixes what was found; this can repeat several rounds. Hand-in is the moment a team declares a building finished at its station and passes it on. LumoPlan is the planning tool that shows which station each building is at and who is working on it. The preprocessing team (an external team, "Packing Sports") does stations 1, 3 and the marks of station 5; the postprocessing modellers do stations 4–7.

2 · What "an agent" means here, and the words used in the ranking

Automation / batch job
A fixed program that does the same thing every time, for many buildings, without a person. No artificial intelligence needed. Example: "run the room solver on every room that arrived tonight".
LLM agent
A program built around a large language model (the technology behind ChatGPT and Claude). It can read messy information, make judgement calls, write text, and use a limited set of tools we give it. Example: "read all the warnings and write a to-do list for the modeller".
Vision model
An AI that recognises things in images. Example: "is this a door or a window?"
Headless
Running software without a screen. Today most of LumoCad's calculations can only run inside the browser window a person has open. "Headless" means the same calculation runs on a server, by itself.
Human in the loop
The agent proposes, a person approves. Every candidate below is designed this way unless it says otherwise.
Confidence score
A number the software attaches to its own result saying how sure it is. Used to decide what can be accepted automatically and what a person must look at.
Triage
Sorting cases by how much they need a person, so people spend their time on the hard ones.

Each candidate carries a kind (batch LLM vision) and a confidence: how sure I am it works with what we already have (high, medium, low). Tier 1 is high, Tier 2 is medium-to-high with one thing to do first, Tier 3 is a pilot.

3 · In short

  • The calculations mostly exist. Room solver, boundary estimation, boundary clean-up, wall generation, floor-plan drawing with 52 built-in checks, room-book table. All of them are ordinary code that needs no screen in principle. But nothing runs them without a person clicking in the browser.
  • Still fully by hand: the room puzzle (station 2), stacking floors, the whole door/window chain of station 5 (mark → merge → match → place → type → details), deciding wall types, dimension lines (by decision), room names and numbers, grouping rooms into flats, ticking QC checklists, matching room-book words to the catalogue, and marking stages done in LumoPlan.
  • The biggest wins are not new calculations. They are: (a) running the existing calculations automatically as soon as data arrives, (b) agents that read the diagnostics those calculations already produce and turn them into short to-do lists, (c) language and vision agents on the judgement steps.
  • Waiting is half the time. For every phase, a building waits about as many days as it is worked on. A building sits a median of 8 workdays just waiting to be picked up for modelling. Agents that prepare, check and route buildings shorten delivery as much as agents that model.
  • We already have a way to run agents. The company's lumoflow-skills repository contains a specification ("lumoagent job flow") for running an agent as a small cloud job with a fixed set of tools and a required output. Two agents already exist on it (floor-plan-audit, qa-agent). The server also supports a special kind of login for organisations, which lets agent changes be told apart from human changes.
  • The Revit guide raises the bar for preprocessing. The architects re-derive floor levels, storey alignment and exterior wall thickness from the point clouds, which preprocessing had already produced. In the all-LumoCad world those outputs must be trusted enough that nobody redoes them. That pushes up the benchmark (T1-5), the alignment and model-versus-cloud checks (T2-10, T2-14) and the floor-level agent (T2-15).

4 · Station by station: what a person does today, what the computer already does

State means: automatic the computer does it and a person checks · assisted the computer proposes, a person chooses and tunes · manual a person does it.

Station 1 · Sorting

automaticwith review
A person todayStarts the room solver for a room, or "auto-sort all rooms" for a floor. Assigns a type to every measurement the software could not classify (called Unknown; the building cannot be handed in while any remain). Throws bad scans away, choosing one of six reasons. Fixes rooms with hand tools: a "magnet" that snaps a scan into place, a fine-tune button, an align-to-target command.
What exists in codeThe solver is self-contained and already runs in a background thread for whole floors, so it could run on a server. It reports several quality numbers per room. There is no "correct answer" data set to measure it against, and its random search is not repeatable (two runs can differ). runStoreyAutosortRoom

Station 2 · Puzzle

manual
A person todayDrags every room out of the display grid into the floor shape. Two helper tools exist, but they only polish rooms that are already within about half a metre of the right place. The person also sets each floor's height by moving a reference slab in the Sections view.
What exists in codeThe floor auto-sort explicitly "does not solve the physical relative layout of all rooms on a floor". The capture app records which door leads to which room; this door graph is shown in side panels but no calculation uses it. Piotr's "floor sorting prototype" in the Q3 key result scored 5 of 10 (not complete). doorLeadsTo, spaceConnections

Station 3 · Boundaries

assisted
A person todayClicks "Estimate room boundaries"; must first draw helper lines (reference planes) along any slanted wall, otherwise those walls silently drop out; runs "fine-tune" with a choice of directions and six sliders; fixes wall-thickness labels one pair at a time; runs "fit boundaries to walls".
What exists in codeAll of it is ordinary code on the browser's main thread. The estimator computes a confidence per room, but the panel shows only counts (how many fitted, fell back, failed).

Station 4 · Walls

automatic geometrytype manual
A person todayClicks "Create boundary walls" (this deletes the floor's existing walls and recreates them, after a confirmation); opens the Wall Type Organizer, which groups walls by thickness and suggests but applies nothing; runs "Match walls to slabs".
What exists in codeGenerated walls carry no type at all. Deciding the type is the one pure judgement step left here.

Station 5 · Doors, windows, stairs

manual chain
A person todayClicks four corners per door/window in every panorama where it is visible; merges duplicate marks of the same door; links each mark to its capture opening in a pop-up; clicks on the wall to place the door (the position is wherever they clicked); assigns a type; sets details such as side lights or glass panels. Stairs: two or three clicks plus typed values.
What exists in codeWhen a panorama is uploaded, a vision model on the server already produces masks (outlines of doors, windows, floor, ceiling and radiators, each with a confidence). LumoCad only draws these as a coloured overlay. A server task can turn the masks into observations, but a person has to start it by hand, and LumoPlan's progress tracking counts its output as "not done by a human". The planned feature "spawn a door from its observation" is marked NOT STARTED. objectTags, PROCESS_OBSERVATION

Station 6 · Floor plan

assisted
A person todayOpens the "Modelling QC" panel (off by default), reads up to 52 kinds of warnings (for example "two walls do not meet", "room has no name", "section line not shown on plan"), fixes the model; clicks "seed room names"; picks a starting room and numbers rooms from it; draws every dimension line by hand (Rio decided to keep this manual); builds the sheets and fills the title block; exports PDF and DXF.
What exists in codeThe plan is produced by one self-contained function and the automated tests already run it without a browser. What is missing is a way to load a building's model outside the browser. Customer delivery is still PDF + DWG from Revit; approval of the LumoCad DXF is pending. prepareFloorPlanDocument

Station 7 · Room book

assisted
A person todayGroups rooms into flats; fixes names; fills the measurement checklists; exports Excel/PDF or the master template. If a checklist value does not match the fixed catalogue words, it is silently dropped (only a developer console message).
What exists in codeThere are two room books (one inside LumoCad, one in the platform's Excel template) and they compute areas differently: only the LumoCad one subtracts for sloped roofs and stairs. The same room can show different numbers in the two.

Quality control

manual
A person todayTicks the 2D checklists (General 9 items, per floor 9, per elevation 7, per section 14) and the 3D checklists (9 lists × 2 levels); ticks the 5 manual hand-in checkboxes (4 more are automatic).
What exists in codeThe 52 plan warnings are not connected to the checklist items. The hand-in checkboxes are stored only in that person's browser. The QC hand-in feature is built but not switched on.

Process (coordinators)

manual
A person todaySets up QC task bundles, marks stages done in LumoPlan, reminds people, shares and unshares buildings, keeps Notion in sync.
What exists in codeThe server does not announce changes (no event feed); LumoPlan has no way for a program to log in; the automatic sharing with the preprocessing team is switched off.
The Revit workflow, for comparison

Two sources: the Notion page "Revit Workflow From Start To End" (Axel, Timothy, Elyes; a step list whose pages are screen recordings) and the Revit production workflow guide, a written version of how the architects build the model today: project setup → levels → drone model → walls → doors and windows → roofs → curtain walls → final checks.

  • They re-derive what preprocessing already produced. Floor levels from the slab surfaces and the stair cloud; storey alignment from exterior walls and the stair core; exterior wall thickness from a visible opening or the drone model. In the all-LumoCad world these outputs must be trusted enough that nobody redoes them.
  • Their alignment rule is concrete: anchor on exterior walls and the stair core, never on furniture; verify two distant edges on both axes. Used below as the acceptance test for T2-1 and T2-10.
  • Doors and windows: width and position from the floor cloud, sill and height from the section cloud, then a check of the configuration (rows, columns, side panel, transom, swing). Used in T1-3, T2-3 and T3-4.
  • Their final checklist is mostly "does the model sit on the cloud". None of LumoCad's 52 plan warnings look at the cloud today. That is T2-14.
  • Roofs and curtain walls take a large share of Revit time. Curtain walls do not exist in LumoCad; roofs stay manual-first by Milton's decision. These are feature gaps, not agent items. LumoCad has a tool for every other step.

5 · Tier 1 — build first

High confidence: the pieces exist in code today and the gain is large. Each card says what the problem is today, what the agent would do, why it is realistic, and what has to happen first.

  1. T1-1

    Readiness & QA report agent

    stations 6 + QCbatchLLM
    The problem todayThe software can already check a plan for 52 kinds of problems, but only when a person opens a panel in the browser and reads the list. QC checkers then tick a separate checklist by hand, without those results.
    What the agent doesEvery night and before each hand-in, run the check for every building on a server. Turn the list into a readable report. For every checklist item that the check can prove ("walls meet cleanly", "all sections are shown on the plan", "windows are at the cut height", "every room is named", "there are dimension lines"), mark it proven or not. Send each problem to the right person. Draft the hand-in note.
    Why it is realisticThe check is plain code; the automated tests already run it without a browser. The 52 warning codes are defined in one file. constants/floorplanWarnings.ts, prepareFloorPlanDocument
    What must happen firstP1, a way to load a building's model on a server (see section 8).
  2. T1-2

    Openings proposer: photo outlines → draft marks

    station 5batch
    The problem todayFor every door and window a person opens each panorama and clicks four corners. Yet a vision model already outlines doors and windows on every panorama when it is uploaded. LumoCad only shows those outlines; a server task can turn them into marks but someone has to start it by hand, and its output is counted as "not done by a human".
    What the agent doesRun that conversion automatically on every new panorama; save the results as drafts stamped as agent work; let the person's existing review screens (clean up, merge, link) be the check.
    Why it is realisticAll the pieces exist. The work is wiring and identity. objectTags, PROCESS_OBSERVATION
    What must happen firstP2 (an identity for the agent). Confirm with Piotr which user the server task writes as today.
  3. T1-3

    Match marks to measured openings

    station 5batch
    The problem todayEach door exists twice: as a mark on the photo (knows where the door is, not how big) and as a hand-measured entry from the capture app (knows width, height and the two rooms, not where in the wall). A person links the two one by one in a pop-up.
    What the agent doesMatch them automatically using the rooms they share, the width, and door-versus-window; the person only confirms.
    Why it is realisticBoth lists exist in the database; this is a matching problem with few candidates per door.
    From the Revit guideArchitects read width and position from the floor cloud and sill and height from the section cloud. The agent can cross-check each hand-measured value against the gap in the cloud at the door, which also settles the unit-ambiguity cases.
    What must happen firstT1-2.
  4. T1-4

    Sorting on arrival + triage

    station 1batchLLM
    The problem todayThe room solver runs only when a person clicks it, in the browser, and for a single room it freezes the screen while it runs. The person then checks every room.
    What the agent doesRun the solver on the server as soon as a room's scans arrive. If the result is weak, try again with other settings (a "high-recall" profile, ignoring the compass, aligning in the order scans were taken, or the heavier 3D reconstruction). Score every room with the quality numbers the solver already reports. Show people only the weak rooms, with the reason.
    Why it is realisticThe solver is self-contained and already runs in a background thread; its summary file exists; the 3D reconstruction can already be requested from the server. runStoreyAutosortRoom, POST /task
    What must happen firstP3, a way to save the result back. Today the only route from outside the browser is uploading a backup file that a person then restores.
  5. T1-5

    Benchmark from hand-in backups

    station 1batch
    The problem todayNobody can say how good the solver is, because there is no set of "correct" rooms to measure it against. But every hand-in automatically uploads a snapshot of the final, human-approved room layout. That is a correct-answer set, for free, growing every week.
    What the job doesCompare solver output with those snapshots, compute the error per room, tune the solver's settings, and derive the thresholds that say "this room is good enough to accept without a person" for T1-4.
    Why it is realisticThe developers' own notes ask for exactly this; no data set is checked in yet.
  6. T1-6

    Helper lines for slanted walls

    station 3batch
    The problem todayBoundary estimation assumes walls meet at right angles. For slanted walls a person must first draw helper lines along them; if forgotten, the slanted walls silently disappear from the boundary.
    What the agent doesFind the slanted wall directions from the scan and propose the helper lines before the estimation runs.
    Why it is realisticA function that clusters wall directions already exists. generateBuildingMainGuidelines()
  7. T1-7

    Issue to-do list agent

    stations 3 + 4LLM
    The problem todayThe boundary and wall tools already compute many diagnostics: why a fit failed, edges that nearly line up but do not, wall pairs whose thickness disagrees with the measured door depth (shown as red and orange labels), reasons a correction was skipped. The person has to find and fix them one at a time.
    What the agent doesRead all diagnostics, write one to-do list with a proposed fix per item using the tools' own commands (align, match angle, make parallel), and offer "fix all red and orange thickness pairs" as one undoable action.
    Why it is realisticEvery diagnostic is already structured data in the app; the one-pair fix exists, only the batching is missing.
  8. T1-8

    Wall-type proposer

    station 4LLM
    The problem todayWalls are generated with a measured thickness but no type (24 cm masonry, 10 cm drywall, and so on). A person groups and assigns types by hand; the organizer only suggests.
    What the agent doesGroup the thicknesses, snap each group to the standard list (365, 425, 300, 240, 175, 115 mm; drywall 75, 100, 125, 150 mm), use the measured door depth and the inside/outside position as hints, propose; the person approves.
    Why it is realisticThe grouping exists; applying is the missing step. This is the only pure judgement step left in station 4.
  9. T1-9

    Door and window type auto-apply

    station 5batch
    The problem todaySame as walls: the organizer suggests door/window types, applies nothing, and duplicate types pile up.
    What the agent doesApply the suggestions, name the types by a convention, merge duplicates. Everything is undoable.
  10. T1-10

    Area reconciliation

    station 7batch
    The problem todayThe two room books compute areas differently; the same room can show different numbers depending on which export the client gets.
    What the agent doesCompare the two, plus the area printed on the plan, for every room, and flag each difference with its cause (roof slope, stair deduction, a manual override).
    Why it is realisticBoth exports can already run outside the browser.
  11. T1-11

    Catalogue word mapper

    station 7LLM
    The problem todayThe room-book template accepts only fixed German catalogue words (for floor coverings and so on). Checklist entries that do not match are silently dropped.
    What the agent doesMap free text to the nearest catalogue word; a person spot-checks.
    Why it is realisticA closed vocabulary is the easiest kind of language task.
  12. T1-12

    Preprocessing progress & triage agent

    processLLM
    The problem todayLumoPlan already computes how far preprocessing has got on each building (sorted? boundaries? openings? observations?), but nobody tells the team why a building was shared with them or what is left, and old shares linger.
    What the agent doesEach day, per building, write a short note: status, gaps (for example "boundaries requested but none drawn", "sorted but no observations", "only agent-made observations"), what to do next; remind or unshare stale buildings.
    Why it is realisticThe queries exist and are proven against production; the gap is summarising and routing.
  13. T1-13

    Processing health agent

    serverbatch
    The problem todayWhen scans upload, server jobs produce derived files (image outlines, aligned clouds, Revit import files, the 3D package). There is no notification when a job fails or never runs; some must be started by hand. A person notices days later.
    What the agent doesCheck that every recent upload has its derived files; re-run the missing ones; summarise failures.
    Why it is realisticAll checks are "does file X exist for upload Y"; re-running is one server call.

6 · Tier 2 — high value, one prerequisite each

  1. T2-1

    Room-puzzle proposer

    station 2batchLLM
    The problem todayDragging rooms into place is the biggest manual step left.
    What the agent doesUse clues that already exist but that no calculation uses: which door leads to which room (from the capture app), door widths and wall thickness, the compass heading per room, matching wall lines across rooms, and the stair and section scans. Propose a layout; verify it with the existing overlap scoring; the person accepts.
    What must happen firstAlign with Piotr's floor-sorting prototype (scored 5 of 10 in Q3) so it is not built twice. Rooms joined only by a door have little overlap, which is the hard case.
    Acceptance rule, from the Revit guideAnchor on exterior walls and the stair core, never on furniture. Accept a floor only when two distant edges line up on both axes.
  2. T2-2

    Boundaries-to-walls batch runner

    stations 3 + 4batch
    What the agent doesRun boundary estimation, clean-up and wall generation per floor on the server; accept the confident rooms automatically; queue the rest with a reason.
    What must happen firstP1.

    Discard-reason proposer

    station 1vision

  12. T2-12

    Automatic north

    station 2batch
    What the agent doesCompute north from the compass data, flag rooms with contradictory readings, and correct for magnetic declination (the difference between magnetic and true north at the building's location, which the software does not apply today).
  13. T2-13

    Exterior wall thickness from the drone model

    station 4batchnew, from the Revit guide
    The problem todayWall generation needs to know how thick the exterior walls are; today a person types one global offset (default 40 cm) and the slab outline follows it. The Revit guide reads exterior thickness from the Airteam drone model (a 3D survey of the outside of the building, made from drone photos).
    What the agent doesLumoCad already shows the drone model in the Section and Staircase views. Measure, per exterior wall, the distance between the room boundary and the façade in the drone model; propose the thickness and the slab outline.
    What must happen firstThe drone model must be aligned to the building (the Q4 work already places it in the Staircase view); not every building has one.
  14. T2-14

    Model-versus-cloud check

    stations 2–5batchnew, from the Revit guide
    The problem todayRevit's final checklist is mostly "does the model sit on the cloud": wall faces on the cloud, levels on the slab surfaces, walls copied between floors corrected to each floor's cloud. None of LumoCad's 52 plan warnings look at the point cloud.
    What the agent doesFor every wall, measure the distance between its face and the nearest cloud dots; for every floor level, the distance to the slab surface in the section cloud; flag outliers and walls that were copied to another floor but not corrected.
    Why it is realisticThe point clouds are reachable through the file API, and the "flush to point cloud" and wall-thickness preview tools already compute these distances inside the browser.
    What must happen firstP1 (model loader) plus loading the clouds on the server.
  15. T2-15

    Floor levels from section and stair clouds

    station 2batchpromoted from Tier 3
    The problem todayFloor heights are set by hand by moving a reference slab. The Revit guide gives the recipe the architects use: in a section view, draw a horizontal plane on each slab surface in the cloud, and use the stair cloud as the vertical link between floors.
    What the agent doesDo the same automatically: find the horizontal floor and ceiling surfaces in the section scans, chain the floors through the staircase scans, propose each floor's height and slab thickness; a person confirms.
    Why it is realisticLumoCad's "Automatic Heights" already reads floor and ceiling edges from section scans for room heights; the missing part is chaining floors and writing the storey elevation.

  • Stairs from the old staircase scans. No input path exists and the geometry is hard.

11 · Glossary

Agent
Software that uses artificial intelligence to do a task and hands the result to a person for review.
API (the server)
The program on Lumoview's servers that stores all building data and that LumoCad, LumoPlan and the capture app talk to.
API key / organisation key
A login for a program rather than a person. An organisation key stamps changes as made by "the organisation", so agent work can be told apart from human work.
Batch job
A program that does the same fixed thing for many buildings without a person.
BGF
Gross floor area: the whole floor including walls.
Boundary
The clean polygon drawn along the inside face of a room's walls. Source of the room's area and of the walls.
Capture app / capture opening
The phone app used on site; a capture opening is a door or window measured by hand there (width, height, the two rooms it connects).
Checker / corrector
The two QC roles: one ticks the checklist, the other fixes what was found.
Confidence score
A number the software attaches to its own result saying how sure it is.
Correlation score (NCC, IoU)
How well two sets of dots overlap, from 0 (not at all) to 1 (perfectly). The room solver maximises it.
DIN 277
The German standard for measuring floor areas.
Door graph
The record of which door leads from which room to which, collected on site.
DXF / DWG
CAD file formats that architects can open and edit (DXF is the open one, DWG is Autodesk's).
Hand-in
The moment a team declares a building finished at its station and passes it on.
Headless
Running software without a screen, on a server.
IMU
The scanner's motion sensor: tilt and rough compass heading.
LLM
Large language model, the technology behind ChatGPT and Claude.
LumoCad
Lumoview's own 3D editor in the browser, where stations 1–7 happen.
LumoPlan
Lumoview's planning tool: which station each building is at, who works on it, hours.
Mask / outline
What a vision model produces on a photo: the pixels that belong to a door, a window, the floor, and so on.
Measurement
One placement of the scanner: one point cloud + one panorama + motion data.
Observation / mark
A door or window marked on a panorama by clicking its four corners.
Panorama
A 360° photo taken from the scanner's position.
Point cloud
The dots a laser scanner produces; each dot is one spot on a surface with its position in space.
Preprocessing / postprocessing
The two halves of the office work: preprocessing = sorting, boundaries and marks (external team); postprocessing = walls, openings, plan, room book (Lumoview modellers).
Raumbuch / room book
The table of all rooms with names, numbers, areas and properties, delivered to the client.
Reference plane
A helper line a person draws along a slanted wall so boundary estimation does not ignore it.
Revit
Autodesk's building-modelling program, used by the architects at Lumoview; still the delivery path for many clients.
Room solver
The calculation that positions the scans of one room so their dots overlap.
Storey elevation
The height of a floor above the ground.
Triage
Sorting cases by how much they need a person.
Unknown measurement
A scan the software could not classify; blocks hand-in until a person assigns a type.
Vision model
An AI that recognises things in images.
Wall type
The construction class of a wall (for example 24 cm masonry, 10 cm drywall); needed by the client and by Revit.
WoFlV
The German living-area regulation: balcony counts 50 %, cellar 0 %, sloped-roof areas partly.
Work item
A task in the platform (for example one QC checklist for one floor), with assignees and a checklist.
