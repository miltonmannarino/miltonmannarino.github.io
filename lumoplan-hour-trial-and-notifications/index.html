HHour-planning trial

The proposal (6 Oct): the board keeps who works on which building and stage on which days, and drops the per-day hour distribution. People log their own hours at day end with no pre-filled number. Overlap warnings stay visible but stop blocking.

Why: per-day hours are planning work the coordinators do not keep up to date, so the plan drifts from reality and every hour view shows numbers nobody trusts. The hours that count are the logged ones, live since July.

The trap in the current code

An assignment's hours field is already optional, and the code reads a missing value in two opposite ways. The board and Coverage's booking logic treat it as a full scheduled day. Every team and capacity view treats it as zero. "Just stop typing hours" is therefore not a trial, it is a split brain. The build has to pick one reading and apply it everywhere, which is question A2.

Reading 1: zero hours

Less code. But the team swimlane drops bookings with zero hours entirely, so it shows absences and nothing else. Coverage shows everyone fully free every day. Capacity strip reads 0 %. The person hours grid, the planned column in My Work, the building page and the effort baseline's planned series all go empty. The measurement planner sees no post-processing load and offers people who are booked on the board.

Reading 2: full day recommended

Nothing goes blank, everything gets coarse. A booked day is a full scheduled day: 8 h for full-time, the Personio schedule for part-time. Swimlane, capacity strip and person grid show a booked day as 100 %, and two bookings on one day as 200 %, which is the overlap warning the proposal keeps. Coverage counts unbooked days as free, which is what it already books today.

What does not change

  • Stage bars and their lengths: they are sized by m² and the target rate, not by people.
  • Logged hours, Personio attendance, the submitted-allocation flow and cost rates.
  • The operations dashboard forecast and cost: m² estimates plus submitted actuals. Planned hours never fed it.
  • Stage done / overdue tracking, milestones, Packing Sports bookings.
  • The hours-reminder e-mail. It reads attendance, not assignments.

What the build does once A is answered

  • H1 a global switch in Settings; assigning writes no hours and skips the fully-booked prompt.
  • H2 hours fields leave the assignment modal, the package bulk modal, the paint palette, time distribution and stage tooltips.
  • H3 shrink, move and paint conflicts become a toast with "keep" as the default.
  • H4 the hour log opens blank; "Use planned" goes, "Use remaining" from Personio stays.
  • Rollback is the switch. Assignments made during the trial have no hours; a script can backfill them as full days if we revert. If the trial sticks, the distribution code is deleted, not flagged forever.

NNotifications

Where it comes from: your request from 27 July for a reminder to the stage's owner one day before its planned end, so the plan stops drifting silently. And the 24 h grace from the 7 Oct sync: an overdue stage escalates by e-mail only after a day.

Today

  • Five e-mail senders, all through one SendGrid helper that sends only in production: stage assigned, measurement date moved, stage marked done (behind a setting that is off), offer accepted, invoice handoff.
  • No sent log, no dedupe, nothing stored in-app. The only in-app signal is the extension-requests bell on the board.
  • No scheduler inside LumoPlan: every server route needs a logged-in user. The hours reminder is a separate scheduled function that reads the database directly and never calls LumoPlan.

Design

  • One notifications collection that is both the in-app feed and the sent log. A bell in the top bar with an unread count and a panel linking into the board and My Work.
  • In-app first. E-mail only where the person is not expected to open LumoPlan that day. Once everyone is on LumoPlan daily, the e-mail layer goes.
  • Every notification has a dedupe key, so a scan can run twice and nothing doubles. Re-planning a stage's end resets the key.
  • The scan reuses the stage chain and completion state the board uses. There is no second definition of overdue.
