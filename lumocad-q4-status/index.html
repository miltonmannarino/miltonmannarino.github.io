Primer

What this is about

LumoCad is Lumoview's 3D modelling tool, running in the browser inside the Lumoview platform. Our modellers (architects) use it to build a 3D model of an existing building from laser scans and photos taken on site: walls, rooms, floors, doors, windows, stairs and roofs. From that model we want to produce everything the customer receives: 2D drawings (floor plans, sections, elevations), area calculations and room books.

Until now part of that output still went through other software. Models were exported to Revit or AutoCAD to clean up drawings and produce room books. The goal of this quarter is to make LumoCad good enough that a building can be delivered without that detour.

For the quarter (Q4 2026) there are four goals, called Key Results (KRs) in our OKR system in Notion. Each KR is split into numbered project results: concrete outcomes with a "done" line. A result is ticked Complete in Notion when that done line is met.

The four depend on each other. Stairs and roofs must be modelled correctly before they can be drawn on a floor plan, and the room book takes its areas from the floor plan and the roof:

Staircasemodel any stair correctly
Roofmodel roofs precisely, incl. their underside
→
Floor plana complete 2D drawing set, exported to PDF and DWG
→
Room bookevery room and flat with its areas, generated from the plan

Where the requests came from. Each item below says who raised it.

Built vs released. Code goes first to development, the branch the team integrates and tests on. It reaches customers (production, cloud.lumoview.com) when development is merged into main. The last release was on 27 October (evening). Everything described on this page is now in production; nothing built is waiting to be released.

Glossary

Terms used on this page

The model
Storey / Geschoss
A floor level of the building (EG = ground floor, OG01 = first floor, UG01 = basement, DG = attic).
Slab
The concrete floor plate of a storey.
Multi-slab
Allowing one storey to have several slabs at different heights (split levels, landings). Piotr's work, live since early October.
Room boundary
The outline of a room on the floor, drawn by the modeller. Room areas come from it.
Room group / flat / Mieteinheit
Rooms grouped into an apartment (rental unit). Created in the Room Book tab.
Drone model
A 3D mesh of the building's outside made by drone photography (OBJ file from the provider Airteam). Used as a reference when modelling roofs.
Drawings
Floor plan mode
The LumoCad mode where 2D drawings are previewed and exported.
Sheet
One drawing page (e.g. A3) in the export.
Section / Schnitt
A drawing of the building cut vertically, showing heights.
Elevation / Ansicht
A drawing of one facade seen from outside (north, south, …).
Title block
The information table at the bottom of every sheet: author, project, drawing name (Planinhalt), storey (Geschoss), scale, BGF, NRF, date.
Dimension chain
The measurement lines with numbers along a wall or facade (Maßkette).
Crop
An outline that limits what part of a plan appears on a sheet, e.g. one flat.
Marketing plan
A sheet showing one flat, used for letting or sales brochures.
DXF / DWG
CAD file formats that AutoCAD and Revit open.
TGA
Building services (Technische Gebäudeausrüstung): lights, sockets, communication devices.
Areas
BGF
Gross floor area (Bruttogrundfläche), measured over the outer walls, per DIN 277.
NRF
Net room area (Nettoraumfläche): the sum of the room floor areas, per DIN 277.
Wohnfläche / WoFlV
Living area under the German Wohnflächenverordnung. Smaller than NRF: floor under sloped ceilings counts fully only from 2 m clear height, half between 1 m and 2 m, not at all below 1 m. Balconies and terraces usually count a quarter (at most half). Stairs with more than three steps are deducted.
Room book / Raumbuch
A table of every level, flat and room with its areas (and often finishes, doors, windows), delivered to the customer.
Roofs
Eave / Traufe
The lower edge of a roof slope. Ridge = top edge. Hip = sloping edge where two faces meet at an outer corner.
Inner roof
The underside of the roof, i.e. the ceiling of attic rooms. Attic heights, Wohnfläche and BGF depend on it.
Dormer
A small structure with a window that sticks out of a sloped roof.
Editable surface
A roof turned into a mesh whose corner points can be dragged freely.
Pick lines
A Revit tool: click an existing wall or edge, type an offset (e.g. 1 m), and the roof edge is placed at exactly that distance.
Stairs
Flight / run
A straight series of steps between two landings.
Landing / Podest
A flat platform between flights.
Winder
Wedge-shaped steps that turn the stair without a landing.
Treppenauge
The gap between the two flights of a U-shaped stair, seen from above.
Walking line
The arrow on a plan showing the direction of travel up the stair.
Opening / void
The hole a stair needs in the slab above it.

2026-Q4 FloorPlan

The goal: a complete, deliverable 2D set produced fully in LumoCad (floor plans, sections, elevations, area plans and marketing plans), exported to PDF and DWG without going through Revit or AutoCAD. The floor plan is also the input for the room book.

Background: LumoCad has generated floor plans, sections and elevations from the 3D model since the summer. On 22 and 30 September Djunejt reviewed real exports and listed what still needed manual clean-up. Most items below come from that review. On 26 October Milton worked through every item that didn't need input from the team: 7 changes, two of them fixes found while testing in the app. They were released on 27 October, so customers now have the new title block and the Flats button.

  1. 1

    Stairs and roofs drawn correctly in 2D

    Partly
    What it's aboutFloor plans are drawn as if the building were cut horizontally about 1 m above the floor. A stair crosses that cut, so drawing conventions apply:
    • the part below the cut is drawn solid, the part above dashed;
    • a break line marks the cut;
    • an arrow (walking line) shows the way up;
    • railings are drawn.
    Roof outlines and walls under sloped ceilings are also drawn dashed. If any of this is wrong, the plan has to be fixed in AutoCAD.Raised by Piotr and Djunejt (early October).
    Built
    • The stair cut is drawn by the conventions: solid below, dashed above, two-line break symbol, walking line, winder steps, and a cross over the slab opening on floors the stair passes through. Built July–August.
    • The roof shows on plans and sections: outline, hatching, slope arrows, parapet, dormers. Built August and September.
    • A dashed "hidden lines" layer exists, but walls under sloped ceilings have to be drawn on it by hand.
    Missing
    • Railings. LumoCad has no railing object at all, so there is nothing to draw. It has to be built in the Staircase OKR first (Staircase #7).
    • Dashed walls under sloped ceilings generated automatically.
    • A check against the stair and roof test sets. Those sets don't exist yet (see Staircase #8, Roof #8).
    To tick itExport the floor plans of the stair test building and the roof test building; Djunejt checks them against drawing conventions.
    Waiting onThe railing model (Staircase #7) and the test sets (Djunejt).

2026-Q4 Roof (including inner roof)

The goal: simple roofs built on a slab already work in one click. Custom roofs lacked precision: points were placed without distances to walls, and editing a surface could create shapes the modeller didn't intend. This quarter makes custom roofs precise and predictable, so the team can model the roofs we meet on site. It also makes the roof's underside reliable, since attic heights, Wohnfläche and BGF are calculated from it.

Background: roof feedback came from Axel and Djunejt on the sprint page and from a meeting with Djunejt in early October. Milton built most of it between 8 and 13 October (45 changes), and it was released on 19 October. Nothing has changed in the roof code since 13 October. The modelling team has not yet confirmed the earlier September feedback round in the app.

  1. 1

    Roof editing never changes other elements

    Ticked, gap
    What it's aboutA serious bug. While dragging the corner points of a roof, Djunejt also moved the outlines of rooms, even on other floors, which damaged the rooms. It had been fixed twice before and kept coming back. The done line asks for an automatic test so it can't return unnoticed.
    Built (8 Oct)The root cause: the selection tool allowed roof points and room points to be selected together, so one drag moved both. Now they can't be mixed. Automatic tests check this on every code change.
    Gap foundOn 12 Oct Piotr added a Show Roof toggle to the room-editing mode. With it on, roof points become selectable there too. The "don't mix" check only runs when adding to an existing selection, so a plain drag-box over a roof corner and a room corner can still select both. Not yet reproduced in the app, but it would explain why the bug "keeps coming back".
    To testRoom-editing mode with Show Roof on: drag a selection box over a roof corner and a room corner, then move it. Only one kind of point may move. Repeat in roof mode.
    Waiting onPiotr: should roof points be editable in that mode at all?
  2. 2

    Predictable rules for editing roof surfaces

    1 of 4
    What it's aboutA roof can be turned into an "editable surface" whose corners can be dragged. If a corner is moved out of its face's plane, the face silently splits into two triangles. That is geometrically valid, but in 3D it still looks flat; only a section reveals the fold. Djunejt proposed rules: points only move along their edges; edges move horizontally; raising or lowering an edge slides it along the neighbouring edges. These have to be agreed with the modelling team after Djunejt looks at how Revit does it (aim: easier than Revit), then implemented, then shown in a walkthrough session. This is the one unsolved design problem of the quarter.
    BuiltFaces that aren't flat are highlighted in 3D (on freeform roofs), so the hidden fold is visible.
    MissingThe rules themselves: points sliding along edges, edges sliding along neighbours, a deliberate choice where a face folds, vertical dragging in 3D, and a written rules document.
    To tick itRules agreed, documented and built, and the walkthrough session with the modelling team held.
    Waiting onDjunejt's rule proposal from his Revit research, and a date for the session.
  3. 3

    Precise roof placement

    Built, test
    What it's aboutWhen drawing a custom roof outline point by point, the modeller got no feedback on distances to walls or the slab edge, so roofs had to be placed by eye or measured by hand. Djunejt asked for Revit's "pick lines" (click a wall, type an overhang such as 1 m, and the eave is placed exactly there), for live distances while drawing, and for alignment like room drawing (horizontal, vertical, 45°). He also asked to choose between following the whole slab outline and following particular walls, because slabs sometimes stick out further than the roof.
    Built
    • Pick lines with an overhang per line.
    • Snapping to wall faces.
    • 45°, horizontal and vertical alignment with Shift to lock.
    • Live length and overhang while drawing; plan views only, not in 3D.
    • Start a roof from the slab outline, from picked walls, or point by point.
    To tick itIn roof mode, top view, on a building where the slab sticks out past the walls:
    • Pick four wall faces, typing 1.0 before one of them: that edge sits exactly 1 m out.
    • Draw point by point and watch the live distances.
    • A 45° edge snaps.
    Done line: "placed to the centimetre without measuring by hand".

2026-Q4 Staircase

The goal: the core stair workflow is good: in recent modelling, the automatic fine-tuning had nothing left to correct. This quarter closes the remaining gaps, so that any staircase we meet can be modelled, edited and exported to 2D without manual rework.

Background: the stair tool was built in stages:

  • a first version in May;
  • Piotr in August–October: winding stairs, attaching to floors or slabs, landings at the start and end;
  • Milton in September–October, from René's and Djunejt's feedback on the sprint page: any sequence of turns and landings, slab openings shaped like the stair, and a choice of underside.

That last part waited for Piotr's multi-slab work and was released on 19 October. Since then, stairs saved without a chosen underside show a smooth 150 mm underside instead of a solid block, so existing stairs look different in 3D.

  1. 1

    Stair work merged and attached to slabs

    Ticked, gap
    What it's aboutThe stair features had been sitting on a separate branch until multi-slab was released. With multi-slab, a stair's bottom and top can attach to a specific slab inside a storey, not just to the storey. Done line: "a stair between two slabs of the same storey models and exports correctly".
    BuiltReleased 19 Oct. Attaching to specific slabs works, and the same-storey case is covered by automated tests, including the IFC export.
    GapNobody has tried the done-line case in the app. The browser test of 19 Oct used three separate storeys.
    To testOn a storey with two slabs at different heights:
    • Add a stair, tick both slabs under Slab connections, and place it. An opening appears in the upper slab only.
    • Export the plan (PDF/DXF) and the IFC model: the stair is there with the right height.
  2. 2

    Stairs that span several floors

    Built, test
    What it's aboutA stair that runs through several floors should repeat its turn pattern on each floor, like a U-stair turning on every level. That behaviour was lost in a code refactor: choosing a higher target floor stretched the stair into one long run instead. One continuous run should stay available on purpose, e.g. for a shopping hall stair from ground floor to second floor (Piotr).
    Built
    • Tick three or more slabs and you get one run per floor, each turning on its own level. Tick only the bottom and top slab and you get one continuous run.
    • Automatic placement ticks only the next slab up, so for the repeating version the in-between slabs have to be ticked by hand.
    • Unclear what happens when floors have different heights.
    To tick itA U-stair over four storeys turns on each floor. Switching to bottom and top only gives one run. Try floors of different heights too.
    Waiting onPiotr: should automatic placement tick the in-between slabs?
